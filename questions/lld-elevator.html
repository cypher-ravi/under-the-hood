Design an Elevator System

A building has a few cars and a column of call buttons on every floor. Someone presses "down" on floor 6, and a second later one car's lamp lights up for them. Behind that is a small amount of code doing three separate jobs: a dispatcher that decides which car answers, a scheduler inside each car that decides in what order it visits its stops, and a state machine that decides what a car may do right now (move, open its doors, sit idle, or ignore everyone because a technician has the key). Keep those three apart and every follow-up the interviewer throws at you becomes a new class instead of a rewrite.

The interview flow is adapted from Hello Interview's delivery framework for a 45-minute round. The breakdown, diagrams, simulation and code on this page are our own.

1

Requirements

about 5 minutes

Elevators invite scope creep: weight sensors, fire codes, motor curves, express zones. Spend the first minutes agreeing on a building small enough to finish and a time model simple enough to test.

Functional: the system should

  • Run N cars in a building with M floors (the demo: 3 cars, floors 0 to 9).
  • Accept hall calls: an up or a down button on a floor. The top floor has only down, the ground floor only up.
  • Accept car calls: a floor button pressed inside one particular car.
  • Assign every hall call to exactly one car, and let the rule for choosing be swapped.
  • Move each car one floor at a time, keep a direction, and open the doors at each stop, then close them and carry on.
  • Show each car's floor and arrow on a display in the lobby.
  • Put a car into maintenance: it stops taking calls, and calls it had promised go to other cars.

Non-functional: the code should

  • Never lose a call: a lit button is always answered, and pressing it twice is still one call.
  • Be fair: no rider waits forever because traffic keeps arriving in the other direction.
  • Be safe under concurrency: buttons are pressed from many threads while cars move.
  • Be deterministic and testable: time advances in ticks you step by hand, not with real sleeps.
  • Be open to new rules (express cars, peak-hour modes) without editing the car's core.

Say what you are leaving out: passenger weight and capacity (mention it, add it as a follow-up), door obstruction sensors, acceleration and motor control, and fire service. One tick = one floor of travel, and doors stay open for one tick. That's crude, but it makes every number on this page something you can check by hand.

2

Core entities and their responsibilities

about 3 minutes

The trap is one big Elevator class holding a list of floors and a while True loop full of if moving_up and not door_open and .... Split by the question each piece answers.

Building

owns the cars; runs the control loop one tick at a time; tracks lit buttons

ElevatorController

the dispatcher: which car answers this hall call?

DispatchStrategy

interface: cost(car, call); NearestCar and LookCost

ElevatorCar

floor, direction, the two stop sets (up, down); the LOOK order of its stops

CarState

Idle, MovingUp, MovingDown, DoorsOpen, Maintenance: what one tick does

Door

open or closed; the one place a sensor would hook in

HallCall / CarCall

button presses as commands, queued and applied by the loop

Dir

UP, DOWN, IDLE (+1, −1, 0), so moving is floor += dir

LobbyDisplay

an observer: shows "3↑" per car

The key split: the controller decides who, the car decides when. Once a car has accepted a hall call, the controller forgets it; the stop just sits in the car's up or down set and the car's own LOOK rule gets to it. That's what lets you swap dispatch rules without touching movement, and change movement without touching dispatch.

Interfaces of the main classes

about 5 minutes

Two signatures carry the design. cost(car, call) is all a dispatch rule has to say, so a new rule is a new class with one method. press(cmd) only puts a command on a queue; nothing a button does ever touches a car directly, which is what makes the threading story short.

class Building(CarListener):
    __init__(floors: int, cars: {name: floor}, strategy: DispatchStrategy)
    press(cmd: Command) -> None              # any thread; only enqueues
    step() -> None                           # control loop: apply inbox, tick each car
    dispatch(call: HallCall, since=None)     # lit already? same call. else assign
    set_service(car: str, on: bool) -> None

class ElevatorController:
    assign(call: HallCall) -> (ElevatorCar | None, served_now: bool)

interface DispatchStrategy:
    cost(car: ElevatorCar, call: HallCall) -> int   # lower wins; ties go to the first name

class ElevatorCar:
    add_stop(floor: int, d: Dir | None) -> bool     # True: doors already open right here
    tick() -> None                                  # self.state.tick(self)
    next_direction() -> Dir                         # LOOK: keep going while work lies ahead
    should_stop() -> bool
    arrive() -> None                                # clear served stops, open doors, notify

abstract CarState:   name: str, accepts_calls: bool, tick(car)
interface Command:  apply(building)
interface CarListener:  on_car_event(car, event: "move" | "state" | "open", info)

Why two stop sets, not one list: a hall call has a direction. "Down at 6" must not be answered by a car passing 6 on its way up, or the rider steps in and goes the wrong way. So a car keeps up (stops to make while rising) and down (while falling). A car call has no direction of its own; it goes in whichever set points from the car toward that floor. Why add_stop returns a bool: if the car is already standing at that floor with doors open in the right direction, the call is answered on the spot and never enters a set.

5

Design patterns, and why

about 5 minutes

Each pattern answers a requirement. If you can't name the requirement, leave the pattern out.

State: what a car does this tick

"May I take calls?", "what happens when the clock ticks?" and "can the doors open?" all have different answers in Idle, MovingUp, DoorsOpen and Maintenance. One class per state, each with its own tick, replaces a nest of flags (is_moving, doors_open, in_service) that can contradict each other. Maintenance is the payoff: accepts_calls = False and a tick that does nothing, and no other code changes.

Watch out: keep the stop sets and LOOK rule in the car, not copied into each state. States decide what happens on a tick; the car's data says where.

Strategy: which car answers

"Let the rule be swapped" is the requirement. The controller asks the strategy for a cost per car and takes the lowest. NearestCar uses distance alone; LookCost counts the floors the car will actually travel, following its current sweep, before it reaches you. Peak-hour rules, energy-saving rules and zoning are more classes.

Not worth it for a single-car building. With one car, there is nothing to choose.

Observer: displays and riders

Cars emit move, state and open events. The lobby display updates its "3↑" on any of them; the building listens for open to turn off lit buttons, record wait times and let waiting riders board. The car never imports a display class.

Watch out: listeners run inside the control loop's tick. Keep them quick; a slow listener stalls every car.

Command: button presses

Every press becomes an object (HallCall, CarCall, SetService) with apply(building). Any thread can put one on the queue; only the control loop applies them. You also get a log you can replay, which is exactly how the walkthrough rebuilds the run when you switch strategy.

Not worth it as undo support. Nobody un-presses an elevator button.

    import queue, threading
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from dataclasses import dataclass
from enum import Enum

class Dir(Enum):
    UP, DOWN, IDLE = 1, -1, 0
ARROW = {Dir.UP: "↑", Dir.DOWN: "↓", Dir.IDLE: "·"}
DWELL = 1                                    # ticks the doors stay open

# ---- Command pattern: a button press is an object; any thread enqueues it,
# ---- and only the control loop applies it
class Command(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def apply(self, building): ...

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class HallCall(Command):                     # on a floor: "I want to go up / down"
    floor: int
    dir: Dir
    def apply(self, b): b.dispatch(self)

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class CarCall(Command):                      # inside a car: "take me to floor n"
    car: str
    floor: int
    def apply(self, b):
        car = b.car(self.car)
        if car.state.accepts_calls: car.add_stop(self.floor)

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Rider(Command):                        # demo helper: a person who presses the hall
    src: int                                 # button, boards, then presses dst inside
    dst: int
    def apply(self, b):
        call = HallCall(self.src, Dir.UP if self.dst > self.src else Dir.DOWN)
        b.riders.setdefault(call, []).append(self.dst)
        b.dispatch(call)

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class SetService(Command):                   # operator key switch: maintenance on / off
    car: str
    on: bool
    def apply(self, b): b.set_service(self.car, self.on)

class Door:
    def __init__(self): self.is_open = False
    def open(self): self.is_open = True
    def close(self): self.is_open = False

# ---- State pattern: what a car does on each tick depends on its state
class CarState(ABC):
    name = ""
    accepts_calls = True
    @abstractmethod
    def tick(self, car): ...

class Idle(CarState):
    name = "Idle"
    def tick(self, car):
        here = car.floor
        if here in car.up or here in car.down:          # a call right where we stand
            car.direction = Dir.UP if here in car.up else Dir.DOWN
            car.arrive()
        elif (d := car.next_direction()) is not Dir.IDLE:
            car.start(d)

class Moving(CarState):
    step = 0
    def tick(self, car):
        car.floor += self.step
        car.notify("move")
        if car.should_stop(): car.arrive()
class MovingUp(Moving):
    name, step = "MovingUp", 1
class MovingDown(Moving):
    name, step = "MovingDown", -1

class DoorsOpen(CarState):
    name = "DoorsOpen"
    def __init__(self): self.left = DWELL
    def tick(self, car):
        self.left -= 1
        if self.left > 0: return
        car.door.close()
        d = car.next_direction()
        if d is Dir.IDLE: car.direction = Dir.IDLE; car.set_state(Idle())
        else: car.start(d)

class Maintenance(CarState):
    name, accepts_calls = "Maintenance", False
    def tick(self, car): pass

class ElevatorCar:
    def __init__(self, name, floor):
        self.name, self.floor, self.direction = name, floor, Dir.IDLE
        self.up, self.down = set(), set()    # floors to stop at on the way up / down
        self.halls = set()                   # hall calls this car has promised to answer
        self.door, self.state, self.listeners = Door(), Idle(), []
    def subscribe(self, listener): self.listeners.append(listener)
    def notify(self, event, info=None):
        for l in self.listeners: l.on_car_event(self, event, info)
    def set_state(self, s): self.state = s; self.notify("state")
    def tick(self): self.state.tick(self)
    def start(self, d):
        self.direction = d
        self.set_state(MovingUp() if d is Dir.UP else MovingDown())
        self.state.tick(self)                # leave this tick
    def above(self): return any(f > self.floor for f in self.up | self.down)
    def below(self): return any(f < self.floor for f in self.up | self.down)
    def next_direction(self):                # LOOK: keep going while work lies ahead
        if self.direction is Dir.UP and self.above(): return Dir.UP
        if self.direction is Dir.DOWN and self.below(): return Dir.DOWN
        return Dir.UP if self.above() else Dir.DOWN if self.below() else Dir.IDLE
    def should_stop(self):
        f = self.floor
        if self.direction is Dir.UP: return f in self.up or (f in self.down and not self.above())
        return f in self.down or (f in self.up and not self.below())
    def add_stop(self, floor, d=None):       # d: a hall call's direction; None = car call
        here = floor == self.floor
        if here and isinstance(self.state, DoorsOpen) and d in (None, self.direction):
            self.state.left = DWELL          # already here, doors open: hold them
            return True                      # served on the spot
        if d is None:
            if here: d = Dir.DOWN if self.direction is Dir.UP else Dir.UP   # just left it
            else: d = Dir.UP if floor > self.floor else Dir.DOWN
        (self.up if d is Dir.UP else self.down).add(floor)
        return False
    def arrive(self):
        f = self.floor
        if self.direction is Dir.UP:
            self.up.discard(f)
            if not self.above() and f in self.down: self.down.discard(f); self.direction = Dir.DOWN
        else:
            self.down.discard(f)
            if not self.below() and f in self.up: self.up.discard(f); self.direction = Dir.UP
        served = {h for h in self.halls
                  if h.floor == f and f not in (self.up if h.dir is Dir.UP else self.down)}
        self.halls -= served
        self.door.open()
        self.set_state(DoorsOpen())
        self.notify("open", served)
    def describe(self):
        stops = [f"↑{f}" for f in sorted(self.up)] + [f"↓{f}" for f in sorted(self.down)[::-1]]
        return f"{self.name}{self.floor}{ARROW[self.direction]} {" ".join([self.state.name] + stops)}"

# ---- Strategy pattern: which car answers a hall call
class DispatchStrategy(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def cost(self, car, call): ...

class NearestCar(DispatchStrategy):
    def cost(self, car, call): return abs(car.floor - call.floor)

class LookCost(DispatchStrategy):            # floors travelled along the car's LOOK sweep
    def cost(self, car, call):
        if car.direction is Dir.IDLE: return abs(car.floor - call.floor)
        s = car.direction.value              # mirror floors so the car always "goes up"
        here, f = car.floor * s, call.floor * s
        stops = {x * s for x in car.up | car.down}
        same = call.dir is car.direction
        if same and (f > here or (f == here and isinstance(car.state, DoorsOpen))):
            return f - here                  # on its way: pick up as it passes
        top = max(stops | {here, f})
        if not same: return (top - here) + (top - f)        # to the end, then back
        low = min(stops | {f})
        return (top - here) + (top - low) + (f - low)       # a whole loop

class ElevatorController:                    # the dispatcher
    def __init__(self, cars, strategy): self.cars, self.strategy = cars, strategy
    def assign(self, call):                  # -> (car or None, served right now?)
        ready = [c for c in self.cars if c.state.accepts_calls]
        if not ready: return None, False
        car = min(ready, key=lambda c: (self.strategy.cost(c, call), c.name))
        if car.add_stop(call.floor, call.dir): return car, True
        car.halls.add(call)
        return car, False

# ---- Observer pattern: displays and the building hear about car events
class CarListener(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def on_car_event(self, car, event, info): ...

class LobbyDisplay(CarListener):             # the "floor and arrow" screen per car
    def __init__(self): self.shown = {}
    def on_car_event(self, car, event, info):
        self.shown[car.name] = f"{car.floor}{ARROW[car.direction]}"

class Building(CarListener):
    def __init__(self, floors, cars, strategy):
        self.floors, self.t = floors, 0
        self.cars = [ElevatorCar(n, f) for n, f in cars.items()]
        self.controller = ElevatorController(self.cars, strategy)
        self.inbox = queue.Queue()           # thread-safe: any thread may press buttons
        self.lit, self.riders, self.pending = {}, {}, []
        self.waits, self.log, self.boarded = [], [], 0
        self.display = LobbyDisplay()
        for c in self.cars: c.subscribe(self); c.subscribe(self.display)
    def car(self, name): return next(c for c in self.cars if c.name == name)
    def press(self, command): self.inbox.put(command)
    def dispatch(self, call, since=None):
        if call in self.lit: return          # button already lit: same call
        self.lit[call] = self.t if since is None else since
        car, now = self.controller.assign(call)
        if car is None:
            self.pending.append(call)
            return self.say(f"hall {call.floor}{ARROW[call.dir]}: no car in service, queued")
        self.say(f"hall {call.floor}{ARROW[call.dir]} -> {car.name}")
        if now: self.served(car, {call})     # its doors were open right there
    def set_service(self, name, on):
        car = self.car(name)
        if not on:
            orphans = sorted(car.halls, key=lambda h: (h.floor, h.dir.value))
            car.halls, car.up, car.down = set(), set(), set()
            car.direction = Dir.IDLE
            car.door.open(); car.set_state(Maintenance())
            self.say(f"{name} to maintenance at {car.floor}")
        else:
            orphans, self.pending = self.pending, []
            car.door.close(); car.set_state(Idle())
            self.say(f"{name} back in service")
        for call in orphans:                 # hand them back to the dispatcher
            self.dispatch(call, since=self.lit.pop(call))
    def on_car_event(self, car, event, info):
        if event == "open": self.served(car, info)
    def served(self, car, calls):
        for call in sorted(calls, key=lambda h: h.dir.value):
            wait = self.t - self.lit.pop(call)
            self.waits.append(wait)
            riders = self.riders.pop(call, [])
            for dst in riders: car.add_stop(dst)
            self.boarded += len(riders)
            self.say(f"{car.name} opens at {call.floor}{ARROW[call.dir]} (waited {wait})"
                     + (f", rider presses {','.join(map(str, riders))}" if riders else ""))
    def say(self, text): self.log.append(text)
    def step(self):                          # one tick of the control loop
        self.t += 1
        while not self.inbox.empty(): self.inbox.get().apply(self)
        for car in self.cars: car.tick()
    def busy(self): return self.lit or any(c.up or c.down for c in self.cars)

# ---------------- demo ----------------
SCRIPT = {1: [CarCall("B", 9)], 2: [Rider(6, 1)], 3: [Rider(2, 7)],
          4: [Rider(8, 0)], 5: [Rider(3, 0)], 6: [SetService("C", False)]}

def run(strategy):
    b = Building(10, {"A": 0, "B": 4, "C": 8}, strategy)
    print(f"== {type(strategy).__name__}: floors 0-9, A@0 B@4 C@8 ==")
    while b.t < max(SCRIPT) or b.busy():
        for cmd in SCRIPT.get(b.t + 1, []): b.press(cmd)     # pressed during the next tick
        seen = len(b.log)
        b.step()
        print(f"t={b.t:<2} " + " | ".join(c.describe() for c in b.cars))
        for line in b.log[seen:]: print("     " + line)
    print(f"waits {b.waits}: mean {sum(b.waits) / len(b.waits):.1f} ticks, max {max(b.waits)}")

run(NearestCar())
run(LookCost())

# eight threads press buttons at once; only the control loop touches the cars
b = Building(10, {"A": 0, "B": 4, "C": 8}, LookCost())
def worker(k):
    for i in range(5):
        src = (k + 3 * i) % 10
        b.press(Rider(src, (src + 1 + (k + i) % 9) % 10))
threads = [threading.Thread(target=worker, args=(k,)) for k in range(8)]
for th in threads: th.start()
for th in threads: th.join()
while b.busy() or not b.inbox.empty(): b.step()
print(f"8 threads, 40 riders: {b.boarded} boarded, {len(b.lit)} buttons still lit")

    8a
    Deep dive: the lit button nobody answers
    about 4 minutes
    
    
    Suppose button threads write straight into a car's stop set. The control loop is clearing floor 7 (the car just arrived there) at the same moment a rider inside presses 9. Both read the set, change their own copy, and write it back. Step through it with no protection, a lock per car, and a command queue.
    
    
    
    
    Ways to make it safe
    
    
      
      
    • A lock per car. Every reader and writer of up, down and halls takes the car's lock. Correct, but the dispatcher has to read every car to compare costs, so it must take all the locks in a fixed order (A, then B, then C) or two dispatches can deadlock.
      • 
      
    • One writer, a queue for everyone else (what the code does). Button threads only call inbox.put, which is thread-safe. The control loop is the only code that ever touches a car, so nothing inside a car needs a lock, and dispatch sees all cars in one consistent tick. The cost: a press waits at most one tick before it's applied.
      • 
      
    • The dispatcher's own race. Even with locks, "compute costs, then add the stop" is two steps; a car can move between them and the cost is stale. In the single-loop design this can't happen, because no car moves while the inbox is being applied.
      • 
      
    • Duplicates. Two people press "down at 6" on different threads. Both commands are queued; the first lights the button, the second finds lit already holds that call and does nothing. That check is safe only because one thread does it.
      • 
    
    
    
    Real hardware agrees: each car usually has its own controller running a fixed scan loop, and a group controller assigns hall calls. Buttons are inputs read once per scan, not callbacks that mutate state. A tick-based loop with a queue is the software version of that, and it's what lets the test above press 40 riders from 8 threads and get the same answer every time.

    8c
    Deep dive: SOLID, and where to stop
    about 2 minutes
    
    
    
      
    Single responsibility
    The controller picks a car, the strategy prices one, the car orders its stops, the state decides a tick. Building does a bit too much (queue, lit buttons, riders, maintenance); a HallPanel class for the lit buttons is the first thing to pull out if it grows.
    
      
    Open/closed
    New dispatch rules, states and listeners are new classes (see 8b). Capacity and express cars edit the core, because they change what "can this car stop here" means.
    
      
    Liskov
    Every strategy must return a cost for any car that accepts calls, never raise, and use the same unit (floors) so ties and comparisons mean something. Every state must leave the car in some state after tick.
    
      
    Interface segregation
    Displays see events, not stop sets. Strategies read a car but never call add_stop. Button threads see only press.
    
      
    Dependency inversion
    The controller depends on DispatchStrategy, the car on CarState and CarListener. Time is ticks driven from outside, so tests never sleep.
    
      
    Where to stop
    No Floor class: a floor is an int until it has behaviour (say, a locked floor needing a badge). No Passenger in the core: riders are a demo helper; the system only ever sees buttons.
    
    
    
  
    

  
    
    Worked example
    
    The rider at floor 6, by hand
    
    
    Cars start idle at A = 0, B = 4, C = 8. In tick 1 someone inside B presses 9, so B leaves for 9 and is at floor 5 going up. In tick 2 a rider at 6 presses down. These numbers match the Python output and the walkthrough.
    
    
      
      
    1. NearestCar prices the call
      Distance only: A = |0 − 6| = 6, B = |5 − 6| = 1, C = |8 − 6| = 2. B wins. It puts 6 in its down set, because the call is "down".
      2. 
      
    2. B passes the rider
      Tick 3: B reaches 6 going up. should_stop: is 6 in up? No. Is 6 in down with nothing above? No, 9 is above. So B keeps going: 7, 8, and 9 at tick 5, doors open for its car call. It turns, and opens at 6 going down at tick 8. The rider waited 6 ticks for a car that was one floor away.
      3. 
      
    3. LookCost prices the same call
      A is idle: 6. C is idle: 2. B is moving up and the call is down, so B must finish its sweep first: up to its highest stop, 9, then back to 6: (9 − 5) + (9 − 6) = 7. C wins with 2; it opens at 6 in tick 3: a wait of 1.
      4. 
      
    4. Tick 5: the rider at 3 going down
      Under LookCost, A (at 2, doors open, heading up to 7) costs (7 − 2) + (7 − 3) = 9; B (at 8, heading up to 9) costs (9 − 8) + (9 − 3) = 7; C (at 5, going down to 1) has 3 on its way: 5 − 3 = 2. C takes it.
      5. 
      
    5. Tick 6: C goes into maintenance
      set_service("C", False) clears C's stops and hands back its promised call 3↓, keeping the original press time (tick 5). Re-priced: A at 3 heading up to 7 costs (7 − 3) + (7 − 3) = 8; B at 9 with doors open costs 0 + (9 − 3) = 6. B answers at tick 11: wait 11 − 5 = 6. Under NearestCar the same rider got A (distance 1 at tick 5), which first rode up to 7: wait 8.
      6. 
    
    
  
    

  
    
    From first principles
    
    What the problem really is
    
    
    
      
    What
    A few state machines (the cars) fed by an assignment rule (the dispatcher), each serving its own set of stops in a sweep.
    
      
    Why it's hard
    Not the movement. It's that the cheapest-looking assignment ignores where a car is already committed to go, and that buttons arrive from everywhere while cars change state.
    
      
    Key insight
    Price a call by the path the car will really take (its sweep), and let exactly one loop change car state, with every press queued as a command.
    
      
    Same shape elsewhere
    Disk-head scheduling (SCAN and LOOK come from there), ride-hailing dispatch, warehouse robots picking orders, and print queues across several printers.
    
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    
      
    Follow-up questions to expect
    
      
      
        
    • SCAN or LOOK? SCAN always runs to the last floor before turning; LOOK turns at the last stop. LOOK wastes fewer floors, and that's what next_direction does.
      • 
        
    • Re-assigning calls: a better car may appear after assignment (another car becomes idle nearby). Re-run dispatch for unanswered calls each tick, but only move a call if it saves more than a margin, or the lamp's car keeps flipping.
      • 
        
    • Maintenance with riders inside: here the car stops where it is. A gentler version finishes its car calls first, takes no new hall calls, and enters Maintenance when its stop sets are empty.
      • 
        
    • Door obstruction: the door reports "blocked"; DoorsOpen.tick resets left instead of closing, with a limit after which it closes slowly and raises an alarm.
      • 
        
    • Measuring a strategy: mean and worst wait (the demo prints both), time in car, and floors travelled (energy). Replay a recorded day of presses against each strategy: the command log makes that free.
      • 
        
    • A 60-floor tower: split into zones served by different car groups, with sky lobbies. Each zone is this design; a rule maps a destination to a zone.
      • 
        
    • Testing: because time is a tick you call, a test presses buttons, calls step() a fixed number of times and asserts exact floors and states.
