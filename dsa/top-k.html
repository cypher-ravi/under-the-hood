Top K and Two Heaps

You rarely need everything sorted. Often you just need the biggest few, or the middle value, and you need it again after every new number. A small heap keeps exactly that, cheaply.

In 30 seconds
  • What: a heap is a structure that always hands you its smallest (or largest) item. Keep one of size k, or two that split the data in half.
  • When: "top k", "kth largest", "k most frequent", merging sorted lists, or a median that must update as numbers arrive.
  • Why it's fast: reading the top is O(1), adding or removing is O(log k). n items cost O(n log k) instead of O(n log n) for a full sort.
  • The one thing to get right: the root must be the item you'd throw away next. So for the k largest, use a min-heap.

Pattern-first structure inspired by Codeintuition's curriculum. Problems, explanations and visualizations on this page are our own.

The idea, by example

Keep the 3 biggest numbers you've seen

Numbers arrive one by one: 4, 9, 1, 7, 3, 8. At every moment we want the 3 biggest so far. We don't sort anything. We just keep a small group of 3 and always know who its weakest member is.

NewGroupWeakestWhat happens
444Room left. Add it.
94 94Room left. Add it.
11 4 91Room left. Add it. The group is now full.
74 7 947 beats the weakest (1). Kick 1 out, let 7 in.
34 7 943 doesn't beat 4. It can't be in the top 3. Ignore it.
87 8 978 beats 4. Kick 4 out. The 3rd largest is 7.

Each new number is compared with one value only: the weakest of the group. We need a container that hands us its smallest item instantly and lets us swap it out quickly. That container is a min-heap.

To keep the best k, you only need to know the worst of them. A min-heap of size k keeps that worst one at the top, ready to be compared or kicked out.

The idea, continued

A heap is a tree packed into an array

A min-heap is a binary tree with two rules. Every level is full except maybe the last, which fills from the left. And every parent is no larger than its children. Left versus right doesn't matter, so it is far from sorted, but the smallest item is always at the top (the root).

Because there are no gaps, it fits in a plain array, row by row. The children of index i sit at 2i + 1 and 2i + 2.

Two repairs keep the parent rule true:

  1. Push: put the new item at the end, then swap it with its parent while it's smaller ("sift up").
  2. Pop: take the root, move the last item to the top, then swap it with its smaller child while that child is smaller ("sift down").

Each repair walks one path from top to bottom, and a heap of n items has about log₂ n rows. So push and pop are O(log n). A max-heap flips the comparison. Python's heapq only has a min-heap, so store -x to fake a max-heap.

Recognizing the pattern

Signals in the problem statement

Reach for a heap when you see one or more of these:

  • The words k largest, k smallest, kth, top k, k closest, k most frequent.
  • You must answer after every new item (a stream), so sorting once at the end isn't an option.
  • You keep needing the smallest of several candidates that change: the fronts of k sorted lists, the meeting that ends first.
  • The answer lives in the middle (a median), or you need the largest of one group and the smallest of another. Two heaps.
  • k is much smaller than n. A size-k heap costs O(n log k) time and O(k) memory.

When not to: if you need the answer once and k is close to n, just sort. If you need it once and memory is tight, quickselect finds the kth largest in O(n) on average, but it can't handle a stream.

The same idea scales up to a whole system. When the stream is too big for one machine to count exactly, see the design question Top K at scale and the count-min sketch.

Template

Two skeletons

The first covers most problems. The second is for "the middle".

Keep the k largest

heap = []           # min-heap
for x in stream:
    heappush(heap, x)
    if len(heap) > k:
        heappop(heap)  # drop weakest
# heap[0] is the kth largest

Two heaps around the middle

low, high = [], []  # low stores -x
def add(x):
    if not low or x <= -low[0]:
        heappush(low, -x)
    else:
        heappush(high, x)
    # low may be 1 bigger, no more
    if len(low) > len(high) + 1:
        heappush(high, -heappop(low))
    elif len(high) > len(low):
        heappush(low, -heappop(high))

To adapt it to a new problem, answer three questions:

  1. What should sit at the root? The item you'd throw away next (top k), or the item you need next (merging). That picks min-heap or max-heap.
  2. What do you rank by? The value, its count, or minus its distance. Push tuples like (count, value): Python compares the first field first.
  3. One heap or two? One for "best k" or "next smallest". Two when the answer is in the middle.
Problem 1 · Keep the best k

Kth largest element

Question

Given a list of integers and a number k, return the kth largest value in the list, counting duplicates separately (so in [5, 5, 4] the 2nd largest is 5). Try to do better than sorting the whole list.

Inputnums = [3, 2, 1, 5, 6, 4], k = 2
Output5

While scanning, what is the least you need to remember?

The obvious way

Sort the list from largest to smallest and take position k. That's O(n log n), and it sorts thousands of values you never look at.

Key insight

Only the k largest values so far matter, and the kth largest is the smallest of them. Keep them in a min-heap of size k. A new value either loses to the root (it can't be in the top k) or beats it (the root drops out). At the end, the root is the answer.

The plan

  1. While the heap has fewer than k items, push.
  2. After that, compare each new value with the root.
  3. Bigger? Replace the root with it. Otherwise skip it.
  4. Return the root.

Watch it run

The top row is the input, with x on the current value. Below it is the heap, drawn both as an array and as a tree. The root is always the current kth largest.

Time O(n log k)Space O(k)Sort everything: O(n log n)

Using a max-heap of size k for the k largest. Its root is the biggest value, so you'd kick out the best item instead of the weakest.

Problem 2 · Keep the best k, by count

K most frequent elements

Question

Given a list of integers and k, return the k values that appear most often, most frequent first. When two values have the same count, either may be chosen; this version keeps the larger value, because it compares (count, value) pairs.

Inputnums = [1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 3], k = 2
Output[1, 2]

This time, what makes a value "big"?

The obvious way

Count each value, then sort all the distinct values by count. With d distinct values that's O(d log d), even when k is 2.

Key insight

Rank by count, not by value. Count everything with a hash map in one pass, then it's Problem 1 again on (count, value) pairs: a min-heap of size k, so the rarest of the current top k sits at the root, ready to be kicked out.

The plan

  1. Count each value with a hash map.
  2. Push each (count, value) pair onto a min-heap.
  3. If the heap grows past k, pop the root (the rarest).
  4. Return the heap's values, most frequent first.

Watch it run

In the heap, the big number is the value and ×c under it is its count. The heap is ordered by count first, then value.

Time O(n + d log k), d = distinct valuesSpace O(d)Bucket sort by count: O(n)

Pushing (value, count) instead of (count, value). Python compares the first field first, so the heap ranks by value and returns the wrong items.

Problem 3 · Smallest of k fronts

Merge k sorted lists

Question

You are given k lists, each already sorted from small to large (some may be empty). Combine them into one sorted list.

Input[[1, 4, 5], [1, 3, 4], [2, 6]]
Output[1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 4, 5, 6]

Where can the next smallest value possibly be?

The obvious way

Glue all the lists together and sort. O(N log N) for N values in total, and it throws away the fact that each list is already sorted.

Key insight

The next smallest value is always at the front of some list, because everything behind a front is at least as big. So you only ever compare k fronts. Keep them in a min-heap; pop the smallest and replace it with the next value from the same list.

The plan

  1. Push the first value of every non-empty list, as (value, list, position).
  2. Pop the smallest and add it to the output.
  3. If its list has a next value, push that.
  4. Repeat until the heap is empty.

Watch it run

In the lists, amber is a front waiting in the heap and crossed out means already output. In the heap, L0, L1, … under a value says which list it came from.

Time O(N log k), N = total valuesSpace O(k) for the heapGlue and sort: O(N log N)

Pushing only the value. When it pops, you no longer know which list to take the next value from. Push (value, list, position).

Problem 4 · Two heaps

Median of a stream

Question

Numbers arrive one at a time. After each one, report the median of everything so far: the middle value when the count is odd, the average of the two middle values when it is even.

Input5, 15, 1, 3
Output5, 10, 5, 4

To report the median, which values do you actually need to see?

The obvious way

Keep a sorted list and insert each number in its place. Finding the spot is fast, but shifting everything after it is O(n) per number.

Key insight

Only the one or two middle values matter. Split the numbers into a lower half and an upper half. The middle is the largest of the lower half and the smallest of the upper half, so keep the lower half in a max-heap and the upper half in a min-heap.

The plan

  1. If x is at most low's top, push it to low. Otherwise push it to high.
  2. If low is more than one bigger than high, move low's top across.
  3. If high is bigger than low, move high's top across.
  4. Odd count: the median is low's top. Even: average the two tops.

Watch it run

low is a max-heap (largest on top) and high is a min-heap (smallest on top). The median is read from their two roots after each number.

add: O(log n)median: O(1)Sorted list: O(n) per number

Putting a new number into whichever heap is smaller just to keep the sizes even. Compare it with low's top first, then rebalance; otherwise a big number can end up in the lower half.

Correctness

Why the heap always holds the right items

For the "keep the best k" heap, the whole argument fits in three sentences:

Always true

After reading some of the input, the heap holds exactly the k largest values seen so far, and its root is the smallest of them.

Each step

A new value bigger than the root belongs in the top k and pushes the root out. A value no bigger than the root doesn't belong. Either way, the claim above still holds.

It ends

Each value causes at most one push and one pop on a heap of size k, each O(log k). n values, O(n log k). With n = 1,000,000 and k = 10 that's about 3 steps per value instead of 20.

Two heaps

Everything in low is at most everything in high, and low has the same size as high or one more. Moving a top across keeps both true, since it's the boundary value. So the tops are always the middle.

In an interview: say what sits at the root and why. "Min-heap of size k, because its root is the weakest of the current top k." Then give O(n log k) and mention sorting and quickselect as the alternatives.

Recap

Cheat sheet

Kth largest

Min-heap of size k. Beat the root or skip. Root is the answer.

K most frequent

Count first, then keep the best k by (count, value).

Merge k lists

Heap of fronts. Pop the smallest, push its next.

Running median

Max-heap low, min-heap high. Rebalance; read the tops.
